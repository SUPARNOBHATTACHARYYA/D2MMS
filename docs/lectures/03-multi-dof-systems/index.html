Lecture 03 · Structural Dynamics & Coupled Mechanics

Multi-DOF Systems: Coupled Equations & Modal Superposition

Assemble global mass $\mathbf{M}$ and stiffness $\mathbf{K}$ matrices for discrete mechanical networks. Formulate the generalized algebraic eigenvalue problem, establish modal orthogonality, and uncouple complex coupled differential equations into independent normal coordinates.

Learning Outcomes
  • Assemble symmetric positive-definite mass and stiffness matrices for multi-storey and multi-body structures.
  • Solve the characteristic determinant $\det(\mathbf{K} - \omega^2 \mathbf{M}) = 0$ for natural frequencies and mode shape vectors.
  • Prove & leverage mass-orthogonality $\mathbf{\Phi}^T \mathbf{M} \mathbf{\Phi} = \mathbf{I}$ to decouple large coupled systems.
  • Compute transient forced responses via modal superposition without inverting coupled differential operators.
01 · Matrix Formulations

3.1 Coupled Matrix Equations of Motion

For an $n$-degree-of-freedom discrete linear structure undergoing small vibrations, dynamic equilibrium in matrix form is written:

$$\mathbf{M} \ddot{\mathbf{x}}(t) + \mathbf{C} \dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) + \mathbf{K} \mathbf{x}(t) = \mathbf{f}(t)$$

Where $\mathbf{M} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ is the positive-definite mass matrix, $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ is the stiffness matrix, and $\mathbf{x}(t) = [x_1(t), \dots, x_n(t)]^T$ is the physical coordinate displacement vector.

Assuming synchronous harmonic free vibration $\mathbf{x}(t) = \mathbf{u} e^{i \omega t}$ yields the generalized algebraic eigenvalue problem:

$$(\mathbf{K} - \omega^2 \mathbf{M}) \mathbf{u} = \mathbf{0} \quad \implies \quad \det(\mathbf{K} - \omega^2 \mathbf{M}) = 0$$

The roots of the characteristic polynomial yield $n$ real natural frequencies $\omega_1 \le \omega_2 \le \dots \le \omega_n$ and $n$ orthogonal mode shape eigenvectors $\mathbf{u}_1, \dots, \mathbf{u}_n$.

02 · Modal Analysis

3.2 Modal Coordinates & Decoupling

Transforming from physical coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ to modal coordinates $\mathbf{q}$ via modal matrix $\mathbf{\Phi} = [\mathbf{u}_1, \dots, \mathbf{u}_n]$.

Substituting $\mathbf{x}(t) = \mathbf{\Phi} \mathbf{q}(t)$ and premultiplying by $\mathbf{\Phi}^T$:

$$\mathbf{\Phi}^T \mathbf{M} \mathbf{\Phi} \ddot{\mathbf{q}} + \mathbf{\Phi}^T \mathbf{K} \mathbf{\Phi} \mathbf{q} = \mathbf{\Phi}^T \mathbf{f}(t)$$

By virtue of mass-normalization ($\mathbf{\Phi}^T \mathbf{M} \mathbf{\Phi} = \mathbf{I}$ and $\mathbf{\Phi}^T \mathbf{K} \mathbf{\Phi} = \operatorname{diag}(\omega_1^2, \dots, \omega_n^2)$), the coupled system separates into $n$ completely independent 1-DOF single-degree modal oscillators:

$$\ddot{q}_r(t) + \omega_r^2 q_r(t) = Q_r(t) \equiv \mathbf{u}_r^T \mathbf{f}(t), \quad r = 1, \dots, n$$
Common Misconception · Do Mode Shapes Represent Absolute Amplitudes?

Students often confuse mode shape eigenvectors $\mathbf{u}_r$ with actual physical displacement magnitudes.

Mode shapes indicate relative shape ratios only, not absolute displacement units. An eigenvector can be scaled by any arbitrary constant ($\alpha \mathbf{u}_r$) and still satisfy $(\mathbf{K} - \omega_r^2 \mathbf{M}) \mathbf{u}_r = \mathbf{0}$. Absolute physical displacement is determined only after combining mode shapes with initial conditions and external force amplitudes through modal participation factors!

04 · Computational Implementation

3.4 Generalized Eigenvalue Solution in Python & MATLAB

Solve for natural frequencies and normalized mode shapes of a 3-DOF shear building structure.

import numpy as np
from scipy.linalg import eigh

# 3-DOF Mass & Stiffness Matrices
M = np.diag([20.0, 15.0, 10.0]) # Mass matrix (kg)
k1, k2, k3 = 10000.0, 8000.0, 5000.0
K = np.array([
    [k1 + k2,   -k2,         0.0],
    [-k2,       k2 + k3,     -k3],
    [0.0,       -k3,         k3 ]
])

# Solve Generalized Eigenvalue Problem: K * v = w^2 * M * v
eigenvalues, Phi = eigh(K, M)
natural_freqs_hz = np.sqrt(eigenvalues) / (2 * np.pi)

print("Natural Frequencies (Hz):", np.round(natural_freqs_hz, 2))
print("Mass-Normalized Mode Shapes Matrix Phi:\n", np.round(Phi, 3))
% MATLAB Generalized Eigenvalue Solution
M = diag([20, 15, 10]);
K = [18000 -8000 0; -8000 13000 -5000; 0 -5000 5000];

[Phi, D] = eig(K, M);
omega = sqrt(diag(D));
freq_hz = omega / (2*pi);

disp('Frequencies (Hz):'); disp(freq_hz);
disp('Mode Shapes:'); disp(Phi);
